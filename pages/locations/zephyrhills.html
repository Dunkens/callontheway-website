AC Capacitor Replacement

When a summer thunderstorm rolls through Pasco County, the power can flicker, and that’s often all it takes to knock out an AC capacitor. A bad capacitor is one of the most common reasons an air conditioner stops cooling, and it’s a frequent call we get in Zephyrhills. Your outdoor unit might be humming, but the fan isn’t spinning, or it might be trying to start and then shutting off. That’s a classic sign of a capacitor that’s given up.

Replacing a capacitor is a simple fix for a trained technician, but it’s dangerous to handle without the right tools and knowledge due to the high voltage stored in the component. We can test and replace your capacitor quickly, often in under an hour, getting your system back to blowing cold air before your home has a chance to heat up. It’s a straightforward repair that makes a huge difference.

Pro-Tip: If your lights flicker and your AC stops working, turn the system off at the thermostat. This prevents the unit from trying to start with a bad capacitor, which can cause further damage to the compressor or fan motor.

AC Contactor Replacement

Another common issue, especially in an area with as much nature as Zephyrhills, is a faulty AC contactor. The contactor is essentially a switch that sends power to your outdoor unit. Because it’s an electrical component, it can wear out over time, but it’s also a favorite spot for ants and other small critters to build nests, causing it to fail. If your outdoor unit is completely silent and not turning on at all, a bad contactor could be the culprit.

We see this all the time in homes that back up to green spaces or have a lot of landscaping. The contactor gets stuck or burned out, and the power simply can’t get to the compressor and fan. It’s a relatively quick and inexpensive part to replace, and our trucks are stocked with them. We’ll get the old one out, put a new one in, and have your system running again in no time.

Pro-Tip: Keep the area around your outdoor AC unit clear of debris, leaves, and overgrown plants. This makes it less attractive to pests and allows for better airflow, which helps all the components last longer.

Blower Motor Replacement

If the air coming out of your vents is weak or non-existent, but you can hear the outdoor unit running, you might have a problem with your indoor blower motor. The blower motor is the fan inside your air handler that pushes the cool air through your ductwork and into your home. When it fails, the air just sits there, and your house gets hot fast, even though the AC is technically “on.”

In Zephyrhills, with its mix of older and newer homes, we see blower motors fail for all sorts of reasons. In older homes, it can simply be age. In newer homes, it can sometimes be a manufacturing defect or an issue with the capacitor that powers the motor. Either way, a dead blower motor means no airflow. We can diagnose the issue, find the right replacement motor for your specific air handler, and get it installed so you can feel the air again.

Pro-Tip: Change your air filter regularly! A clogged air filter is the #1 cause of premature blower motor failure. It forces the motor to work harder to pull air through, leading to overheating and burnout.

Condenser Fan Motor Repair

Your outdoor unit has a large fan on top that’s responsible for getting rid of the heat that’s been pulled out of your house. If that fan stops spinning, the heat gets trapped, and your AC will quickly overheat and shut down. A common reason for this is a failed condenser fan motor. You might hear the compressor trying to run, but the fan blade isn’t moving, or it’s spinning very slowly.

This is a repair you can’t put off. Running your AC with a broken condenser fan can cause catastrophic damage to the compressor, which is the most expensive part of your system. We get calls for this all the time, especially during the hottest days of summer when these motors are working their hardest. We’ll get that motor replaced so your system can breathe again.

Pro-Tip: If you look at your outdoor unit and the fan isn’t spinning but you hear a buzzing or humming noise, turn the AC off immediately and call for service. This can prevent a much more expensive repair.

Refrigerant Leak Repair

If your AC is running but the air isn’t as cold as it used to be, or if you see ice forming on the copper lines, you might have a refrigerant leak. Refrigerant, often called Freon, is the lifeblood of your air conditioner. It’s what absorbs the heat from inside your home and moves it outside. When it leaks out, your system’s cooling capacity drops dramatically.

Finding and fixing a refrigerant leak requires specialized equipment. It’s not as simple as just “topping it off.” We have to find the source of the leak, repair it, and then recharge the system to the manufacturer’s exact specifications. We’re fixin’ to get it done right the first time, so you’re not calling us back a few months later with the same problem.

Pro-Tip: Don’t ignore the signs of a refrigerant leak. Continuing to run your AC low on refrigerant can damage the compressor and lead to a much more costly repair. If it’s not cooling like it should, get it checked out.

Compressor Replacement

The compressor is the heart of your air conditioning system. It’s a powerful pump that circulates the refrigerant, and it’s the component that uses the most electricity. When a compressor fails, it’s a major repair. You might hear a loud, clanking noise from your outdoor unit, or it might just refuse to turn on at all. It’s often the most expensive repair an AC system can have.

In many cases, especially with older systems, replacing the compressor might not be the most cost-effective solution. It can sometimes make more sense to replace the entire outdoor unit or even the full system. We’ll give you a straightforward, honest assessment. We’ll price out the compressor replacement and compare it to the cost of a new unit, so you can make an informed decision without any sales pressure.

Pro-Tip: Annual maintenance is the best way to extend the life of your compressor. Keeping refrigerant levels correct, coils clean, and electrical components in good shape reduces the strain on the compressor and helps it last for years.

Drain Line Cleaning

As your AC cools your home, it also pulls a massive amount of humidity out of the air. That water has to go somewhere, and it exits through a condensate drain line. Here in humid Zephyrhills, that drain line can easily get clogged with algae, mold, and sludge. When it does, the water backs up, and it can cause your system to shut down or, even worse, overflow and cause water damage to your home.

A clogged drain line is a simple problem with potentially serious consequences. We use specialized tools to blow out the drain line and clear any blockages, and we can apply algaecide tablets to help prevent future growth. It’s a quick and easy maintenance task that can save you from a major headache down the road.

Pro-Tip: Once a month, pour a cup of distilled white vinegar down your condensate drain line access port. This helps to kill algae and keep the line clear between professional maintenance visits.
