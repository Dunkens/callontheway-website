Deep Dive: What is a True HEPA Filter?

HEPA stands for High-Efficiency Particulate Air. It's not a brand name; it's a government standard. A true HEPA filter is certified to capture 99.97% of all airborne particles down to 0.3 microns in size. This is incredibly small. To put it in perspective, a human hair is about 50-70 microns thick. A HEPA filter can trap dust, pollen, pet dander, mold spores, bacteria, and even many viruses.

Pro-Tip: Be wary of products marketed as "HEPA-type" or "HEPA-like." These are not certified and do not meet the same performance standard as a true HEPA filter.

Deep Dive: Why a Bypass Installation is Crucial

You can't just stick a thick HEPA filter into your normal filter slot. It's so dense that it would choke your AC system, restricting airflow and causing serious damage. Instead, we install it in a "bypass" configuration. The system taps into your ductwork, pulls a portion of the air out, sends it through the HEPA filter for an intense scrubbing, and then returns the ultra-clean air back into circulation. Over time, this process cleans all the air in your home without straining your AC.

Pro-Tip: A bypass HEPA system is the professional and only correct way to add hospital-grade filtration to a residential HVAC system.

Check This Before You Call

A whole-home HEPA system is a significant investment in your health. Before you decide, consider trying a high-quality standard filter, such as one with a MERV 11 or MERV 13 rating. For many people, this provides a substantial improvement in air quality. If you try a high-MERV filter and still find you need more relief, then a HEPA system is the logical next step.
