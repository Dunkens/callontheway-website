Signs Your System Needs Defrost Board Repair

Ice Buildup on the Outdoor Unit

This is the most common symptom. A thick layer of ice on the outdoor coils that doesn't melt away is a clear sign the defrost cycle isn't working.

System Blows Cold Air in Heat Mode

If the outdoor unit is frozen, it can't absorb heat from the outside air. This results in cold air blowing from your vents, even when the heat is on.

Strange Noises

You might hear loud banging or clanking sounds as the fan blades hit the ice buildup on the outdoor unit. It ain't working right if it sounds like that.

System is Stuck in Defrost Mode

Sometimes the board fails and gets stuck in the defrost cycle, causing the system to run in cooling mode when it should be heating.

Deep Dive: Testing the Defrost Sensors

The defrost board relies on sensors to know when to start and stop the defrost cycle. One sensor measures the temperature of the outdoor coil, and another measures the outdoor air temperature. If either of these sensors is faulty, the board won't get the right information. We'll test these sensors to make sure they are reading the correct temperatures.

Pro-Tip: Keeping your outdoor unit clean can help the sensors get an accurate reading. Make sure the unit is free of leaves, dirt, and other debris.

Deep Dive: Inspecting the Defrost Control Board

If the sensors are working correctly, the problem is likely with the defrost control board itself. We'll visually inspect the board for any burn marks, loose connections, or other signs of damage. We can also test the relays on the board to see if they are sending power to the right components at the right time. A worn out board is a common issue.

Pro-Tip: Power surges from Tampa's frequent thunderstorms are a major cause of defrost board failure. A whole-home surge protector can help protect your HVAC system and other electronics.

Why Tampa's Climate Wrecks Defrost Boards

While we don't get the extreme winters of up north, Tampa's cool, damp winter mornings are the perfect conditions for frost to form on a heat pump. This means our defrost cycles run more often than you might think. The high humidity and salty air can also lead to corrosion on the sensitive electronic components of the defrost board, causing it to fail prematurely.

Check This Before You Call

If you see ice on your outdoor unit, there's one thing you can do. Switch your system to its emergency heat setting (if you have one) to get some warmth in your house. Then, turn the system off completely and let the ice melt on its own. If the ice returns as soon as you turn the heat pump back on, it's time to call us. Don't try to chip the ice off, as you can easily damage the fragile coils.
