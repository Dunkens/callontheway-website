Signs Your System Needs a Capacitor Replacement

Humming or Buzzing Noise

This is the classic sign. The unit is trying to start, but the motor is just humming because the capacitor can't give it that initial push.

AC or Heat Won't Turn On

The system might click, but the outdoor unit never fully kicks on. The fan might not spin, and the compressor won't start.

System Starts Slowly

If the capacitor is weak but not completely dead, the fan motor might start up very slowly before getting to full speed. It ain't working right if it's struggling to start.

AC Shuts Off Randomly

A failing capacitor can cause the motor to overheat, which will trip a safety switch and shut the unit down.

Deep Dive: What is a Capacitor?

A capacitor is like a small battery that provides a large burst of energy to get a motor spinning. Your heat pump has at least two: one for the compressor (the start capacitor) and one for the condenser fan motor (the run capacitor). They are essential for getting the two most important parts of your outdoor unit started and keeping them running smoothly.

Pro-Tip: Never touch a capacitor without proper training. They can hold a powerful and dangerous electrical charge even when the power to the unit is turned off.

Deep Dive: Testing the Capacitor with a Multimeter

To confirm a capacitor is bad, we use a multimeter set to measure microfarads (µF). Each capacitor has a rating printed on its side (e.g., 45/5 µF). We'll compare the reading on our meter to the rating on the capacitor. If the reading is weak or outside the acceptable range (usually +/- 6%), we know the capacitor is worn out and needs to be replaced.

Pro-Tip: A capacitor that is visibly bulging at the top or leaking an oily substance has definitely failed and needs to be replaced immediately, even if the unit is still running.

Why Tampa's Climate Wrecks Capacitors

Capacitors are the number one part to fail on an AC unit in Florida, and it's all because of the heat and relentless use. The extreme heat in a Tampa summer causes the capacitor's internal components to break down over time. Add in the stress from our frequent thunderstorms and power surges, and you have a recipe for a short lifespan. Because your heat pump runs nearly year-round, these components simply get worn out from constant starting and stopping.

Check This Before You Call

If your heat pump isn't starting, the first thing to do is check the circuit breaker. Find the breaker for your outdoor unit and see if it has tripped. If it has, you can try resetting it ONCE. If it immediately trips again, do not reset it again. This indicates a serious electrical problem, very likely a failed capacitor, and it's time to call a professional.
