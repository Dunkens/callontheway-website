What's Included in Our AC Tune-Up

We don't just change your filter and call it done. Here's our complete 20-point inspection and tune-up:

Outdoor Unit Inspection

  • Clean condenser coils (where dirt kills efficiency)
  • Check refrigerant levels with digital gauges
  • Inspect compressor for unusual sounds or vibration
  • Test capacitor and contactor for wear
  • Clean debris from around the unit
  • Check fan motor and blade for damage
  • Inspect electrical connections and tighten if needed

Indoor Unit Inspection

  • Check evaporator coil for dirt buildup
  • Test blower motor operation
  • Measure airflow across the coil
  • Inspect condensate drain line and flush if needed
  • Check air filter and replace if dirty
  • Test safety switches and overflow protection

System Performance Testing

  • Measure temperature split (difference between supply and return air)
  • Check refrigerant pressures and temperatures
  • Test thermostat calibration and operation
  • Verify proper cycle times
  • Measure amp draw on motors
  • Check for proper dehumidification

Takes about an hour, sometimes 90 minutes if we find issues that need addressing.

Why AC Tune-Ups Matter in Tampa

Tampa's climate is brutal on AC systems. Here's what happens without regular maintenance:

Your System Runs 6+ Months Straight

May through October, your AC barely stops. That constant runtime means dirt accumulates fast, parts wear out faster, and small issues become big problems. A tune-up catches wear before it causes failure.

Humidity Clogs Everything

Tampa's 75% humidity creates the perfect environment for mold and algae in your condensate drain line. Without annual cleaning, that line backs up and floods your air handler or ceiling. We've seen thousands of dollars in water damage from a clogged drain that's easy to prevent.

Dirty Coils Kill Efficiency

Tampa's got pollen, dust, oak pollen, and salt air if you're near the coast. All that gunk builds up on your outdoor coil. A dirty coil makes your compressor work 20-30% harder and burns way more electricity. Clean coils = lower bills.

Lightning Damages Electrical Components

Tampa's the lightning capital of North America. Power surges weaken capacitors and contactors even when lightning doesn't directly hit. We replace more capacitors in Tampa than anywhere else—they're stressed by constant surges.

Deep Dive: Refrigerant Check and Adjustment

Refrigerant is what actually cools your air. The system's charged at the factory with a specific amount, and it should stay at that level forever—unless there's a leak.

During a tune-up, we check refrigerant levels using digital gauges that measure exact pressures and temperatures. We're not eyeballing it—we're comparing readings to manufacturer specifications for your specific system running in Tampa's conditions.

What We're Looking For

  • Low refrigerant (indicates a leak)
  • High refrigerant (someone overcharged it)
  • Proper superheat and subcooling (tells us the charge is exact)

If refrigerant's low, that means you have a leak somewhere. We don't just "top it off"—that's wasting money and hurting the environment. We find the leak, fix it, test it, then recharge the system properly.

Pro-Tip: If a company offers to "top off" your refrigerant every year without finding the leak, they're ripping you off. Refrigerant doesn't get "used up"—if it's low, something's leaking.

Deep Dive: Condensate Drain Line Cleaning

Your AC pulls moisture out of Tampa's humid air—gallons per day in summer. That water drips into a drain pan and flows out through a PVC drain line.

Problem is, Tampa's humidity creates perfect conditions for algae and mold growth inside that line. Over time, the line clogs completely. Water backs up, overflows the pan, and floods your air handler or leaks through your ceiling.

We've seen homeowners with serious water damage from a clogged drain line. All completely preventable with regular maintenance during a tune-up.

What We Do

Flush the line with compressed air or a shop vac, then treat it with algae tablets or solution to prevent regrowth. Takes 10 minutes and prevents potential water damage.

Pro-Tip: Between tune-ups, pour a cup of white vinegar down your drain line every month. Keeps algae from building up and prevents clogs.

Deep Dive: Coil Cleaning for Maximum Efficiency

Your outdoor condenser coil releases the heat your AC pulled from inside your house. When that coil's dirty, heat can't escape efficiently, so your compressor works way harder trying to push heat through the gunk.

In Tampa, coils get filthy fast. Pollen in spring, dust year-round, grass clippings from mowers, cottonwood seeds—it all sticks to the coil. Add in salt air if you're near the coast, and you've got corrosion starting too.

What We Do

Carefully clean the coil using low-pressure water or specialized coil cleaner. We work from the inside out so we don't push dirt deeper into the fins. Bent fins get straightened with a fin comb.

Clean coils drop your compressor amp draw by 20-30%. That's real money on your electric bill every single month.

Pro-Tip: Keep grass clippings, leaves, and bushes at least 2 feet away from your outdoor unit. Airflow matters—blocking it with shrubs kills efficiency even with clean coils.

Signs Your AC Needs a Tune-Up NOW

Most tune-ups are preventive, but sometimes your system's telling you it needs help:

Electric Bills Jumped Without Explanation

If your summer bills are 20-30% higher than last year and your usage hasn't changed, your system's working too hard. Usually means dirty coils or low refrigerant.

Weak Airflow From Vents

Could be a dirty filter (easy fix), but might also be a failing blower motor or clogged evaporator coil. Either way, a tune-up will diagnose it.

House Feels Humid Even With AC Running

Your AC should remove moisture. If it's not, something's wrong—could be low refrigerant, oversized system short-cycling, or dirty coils.

Strange Noises From Indoor or Outdoor Unit

Squealing, grinding, or banging sounds mean something's wearing out. Catch it during a tune-up before it breaks completely.
