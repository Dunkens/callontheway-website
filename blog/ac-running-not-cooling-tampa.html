AC Running but Not Cooling in Tampa? What to Check

September 19, 2026 • 7 min read • Reviewed by Dustin Miller

Direct Answer

If your AC is running but your Tampa home is not getting cooler, first confirm the thermostat is set to Cool, replace a visibly dirty filter, check that supply vents and returns are open, and look for ice or obvious blockage around the outdoor unit. If cooling does not return, the system needs measurements—not guesswork—to identify an airflow, refrigerant, electrical, or mechanical problem.

The sound of the system running can be misleading. The indoor blower may move air even when the outdoor unit is not rejecting heat, the evaporator coil is frozen, or airflow is too restricted for normal cooling. Tampa’s long cooling season can make a small performance problem noticeable quickly, but the same symptom can come from several different causes.

The goal of this checklist is not to diagnose refrigerant or electrical components from the living room. It is to separate simple settings from conditions that warrant professional AC repair.

Start With These Six Checks

  • Thermostat: Set the system to Cool and place the setpoint below the current room temperature.
  • Fan setting: Use Auto rather than On so blower operation is easier to interpret.
  • Air filter: Replace a heavily loaded filter with the correct size and type.
  • Supply and return airflow: Confirm vents are open and returns are not blocked by furniture or stored items.
  • Outdoor unit: Look from outside for heavy debris, a fan that is not turning, or unusual noise. Do not remove panels.
  • Ice or water: If you see ice on the copper line or indoor equipment, turn cooling off and arrange an evaluation.

ENERGY STAR recommends checking thermostat settings, inspecting the condensate drain, cleaning the evaporator and condenser coils, checking refrigerant level, and cleaning or adjusting blower components as part of professional maintenance. It also advises homeowners to inspect, clean, or replace the air filter monthly.[1]

Why the Blower Can Run Without Producing Cold Air

A split air-conditioning system depends on indoor and outdoor components working together. The indoor blower moves household air across the evaporator coil. Refrigerant carries absorbed heat to the outdoor unit, where the compressor and condenser release that heat. The U.S. Department of Energy’s Building America Solution Center describes this refrigeration cycle and emphasizes that airflow, matched equipment, and correct refrigerant charge affect performance.[2]

If the thermostat calls for cooling but only the blower is operating, the air from the vents may feel room-temperature. A failed capacitor, contactor, condenser-fan motor, control, or compressor can prevent the outdoor side from operating normally. Those possibilities require electrical testing and system measurements. On The Way has dedicated service pages explaining AC capacitor replacement, AC contactor replacement, condenser-fan motor repair, and compressor replacement, but a symptom alone cannot determine which component failed.

Airflow Problems Can Look Like a Refrigerant Problem

A dirty filter or blocked return reduces the amount of warm household air crossing the indoor coil. If airflow falls far enough, the coil temperature can drop below freezing and moisture on the coil can turn to ice. Cooling then becomes weaker, even though the blower may continue running.

If you find ice, do not keep lowering the thermostat. Turn cooling off, leave the equipment closed, and arrange an evaluation. The technician can determine whether restricted airflow, blower performance, coil condition, controls, or refrigerant charge caused the freeze-up. If the issue involves the indoor coil, see our information about evaporator-coil replacement.

Do not treat refrigerant as a routine top-off

DOE guidance states that correct refrigerant charging is critical to system performance and that refrigerant work must be completed by an EPA-certified technician.[2] If a charge is incorrect, the reason needs to be evaluated rather than assuming that adding refrigerant is ordinary maintenance.

What the Outdoor Unit Can Tell You

From outside the cabinet, look for leaves, landscaping, or stored items that restrict airflow. You can also listen for a normal fan sound versus humming, clicking, grinding, or silence. Do not reach through the grille, open electrical panels, or attempt to reset a breaker repeatedly.

A condenser coated with dirt cannot release heat as effectively. ENERGY STAR notes that dirty evaporator and condenser coils reduce cooling ability, lengthen run time, increase operating cost, and shorten equipment life.[1] Surface debris around the cabinet can be cleared without opening the equipment, but coil cleaning and component testing belong in a professional service visit.

When the Problem Is Only in One Room

If most of the home cools but one room stays warm, the central refrigeration cycle may be working. The issue could involve a closed register, blocked return path, damaged or disconnected duct, room exposure, insulation, or the amount of air delivered to that part of the home. A duct or airflow problem requires a different repair path than a no-cooling failure affecting the whole house.

Uneven rooms are also a reason not to assume that a larger replacement system will solve the problem. Our Tampa AC sizing and Manual J guide explains why equipment capacity, room-by-room load, and duct delivery need to be considered together.

When to Call for AC Repair

Arrange service when the thermostat and filter checks do not restore cooling, the outdoor unit does not operate normally, ice returns after thawing, the breaker trips again, the system makes a new mechanical sound, or the indoor temperature continues to rise. A technician can compare temperatures, airflow, electrical readings, refrigerant-circuit conditions, and control operation to isolate the cause.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my AC running but the house is not getting cooler?

Common possibilities include an incorrect thermostat setting, restricted airflow from a dirty filter, a blocked outdoor coil, a frozen evaporator coil, a refrigerant problem, or a failed electrical or mechanical component. A technician needs measurements to identify the actual cause.

Should I turn my AC off if it is running but not cooling?

Turn cooling off if you see ice, hear unusual mechanical sounds, smell overheating electrical material, or the indoor temperature keeps rising while the system runs. Leaving a malfunctioning system on can add strain and may complicate the repair.

Can a dirty air filter make an AC run without cooling well?

Yes. A heavily loaded filter can restrict airflow across the indoor coil. ENERGY STAR recommends inspecting, cleaning, or replacing HVAC filters monthly and notes that dirty filters can increase operating costs and contribute to equipment damage.[1]

Does low refrigerant mean my AC needs a recharge?

Low refrigerant indicates that the system charge or sealed circuit needs professional evaluation. Refrigerant work should include determining why the charge is incorrect rather than treating repeated additions as routine maintenance.
