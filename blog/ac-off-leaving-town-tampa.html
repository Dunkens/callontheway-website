Should You Turn Off the AC When You Leave Town in Tampa?

Quick Answer

Usually no. In Florida, an AC that never runs removes no moisture, and Florida Power & Light advises against turning it off while you are away. Research found very high away settings such as 85°F often did not control humidity. No single setting fits every home, so keep the fan on Auto and ask a technician about yours.

November 25, 2026 • 6 min read • By On The Way Heating & Air
Editorial source review: On The Way Heating & Air

Why Turning the AC Fully Off Can Backfire in Florida

Leaving for a holiday trip raises a practical question: should the AC go off, or just run less? In Tampa the answer is rarely a single thermostat number, because humidity matters alongside temperature in an empty house. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says moisture control is the key to mold control and recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. [1]

Florida Power & Light's energy expert advises against turning the A/C off while you are gone. FPL says setting it too low wastes energy, while setting it too high can create humidity problems. [2] Late November in Tampa can still bring muggy days between cold fronts, so an AC that never runs may leave moisture-laden air sitting in the house.

What Does Research Say About Away Settings?

Utility starting points and research on long vacancies address different conditions. A single away temperature cannot cover every home.

  • FPL guidance: about 80°F for short trips of a few days and about 82°F for longer vacations. Treat these as starting points to check against measured humidity, not Tampa-specific settings. [2]
  • Florida Solar Energy Center research: in a study of three Brevard County homes, a thermostat setting of 85°F was judged “not effective” for humidity control because the AC ran too little, especially on cool or cloudy days. A setting of 83°F was not reliable in every house, particularly in fall. In some test periods the AC never ran at all. [3]

The study has limits you should know about. It was published in 2005, tested older homes in Brevard County, and focused on long vacancies such as seasonal residents rather than a few days away. Treat it as evidence about how humidity behaves in a closed-up Florida house, not as a rule for your trip. Its core finding still matters: if the thermostat is set so high that the AC rarely starts, it removes little moisture.

Why a Higher Setting Does Not Always Mean Less Humidity

An AC removes moisture while it is running and cooling. When the setting is high enough that the system rarely cycles, there is little dehumidification. The same study found that homes with leakier envelopes needed more moisture removal than tighter homes, so two houses on the same street can behave differently. [3]

The study also found that running the AC for a couple of early-morning hours at a low setting, using a dehumidifier on a timer, and other approaches could control humidity in many of the test homes, with trade-offs in energy use. These are research strategies, not instructions for your equipment. A system with a refrigerant, airflow or duct problem may not produce the cold supply air that some approaches assume. [3] For a deeper look at how humidity and cooling interact, see our explanation of why a Tampa home feels humid even with the AC running.

How Long You Will Be Gone Changes the Answer

A long weekend and a month away are different risks. For a few days, a moderate away setting that still lets the AC cycle is a reasonable discussion with your installer. For weeks or months, the research and FPL both point toward active humidity control, such as a dehumidifier or cycling the AC, instead of letting the house float. FPL suggests cycling the A/C to keep humidity below 60 percent, using a dehumidifier and keeping interior doors open for seasonal absences. [2]

Whatever you choose, keep the thermostat fan on Auto. The FSEC researchers recommend the Auto fan setting in a vacant home to save energy, avoid drawing humid air in through duct leaks and keep the filter from fouling quickly. [3] Our article on whether the AC fan should be Auto or On explains that setting in more detail.

What Should You Do Before You Leave?

  1. Check the filter. A clean filter helps airflow while the system runs unattended. FPL lists replacing the filter before a trip. [2]
  2. Look at the drain. EPA advises keeping air conditioning drip pans clean and drain lines unobstructed and flowing properly. [1] A clogged drain can overflow with nobody home to notice. If it has clogged before, read about why an AC drain line keeps clogging, and consider condensate drain cleaning before you travel.
  3. Clear the outdoor unit. Remove leaves and debris around the condenser, as FPL suggests. [2]
  4. Open interior doors. The FSEC authors advise opening all interior doors before leaving a home vacant so air can circulate. [3]
  5. Add a humidity reading. EPA notes that a humidity meter is an inexpensive way to measure relative humidity. A reading you can check remotely beats a guess, though two meters can disagree, as we explain in why two humidity meters show different readings. [1]
  6. Consider a remote-capable thermostat. FPL mentions a smart thermostat for remote adjustments. Our smart vs. programmable thermostat guide compares the options.

When Should You Not Rely on These Settings?

Arrange service before departure if the system is leaking water or showing other signs of trouble. EPA warns against running an HVAC system that you know or suspect is contaminated with mold, because it could spread mold through the building. [1] If you already see condensation on windows, notice a musty smell, or have had water from the unit, deal with that before you travel. EPA says condensation on windows, walls or pipes can signal high humidity, and it advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. [1]

Also remember that this article cannot choose a setting for your house. Home size, envelope tightness, system condition and weather all matter. A qualified technician can discuss humidity-based controls or a dehumidifier for your situation, and our whole-home dehumidifier installation page describes that service.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I turn my AC off when I go on vacation in Florida?

Florida Power & Light advises against turning the A/C off while you are away. In a humid climate, an AC that never runs removes no moisture, which can raise the risk of mold and mildew in a closed-up house.

Is 85 degrees a good AC setting while I am away?

It is common advice, but a Florida Solar Energy Center study found 85°F ineffective for humidity control in the test homes because the AC ran too little. Results vary by house, so ask a technician about the right balance for yours.

Should I leave the fan on Auto or On while I am away?

The FSEC researchers recommend Auto in a vacant home. It saves energy, avoids pulling humid air through duct leaks and keeps the filter from fouling quickly.
