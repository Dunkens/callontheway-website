How to Lower Your Energy Bill in Tampa: 7 HVAC Tips That Actually Work

Published on February 14, 2026

In the heart of Tampa, with our beautiful but relentlessly hot and humid climate, running the air conditioner isn't a luxury—it's a necessity. But that necessity often comes with a shocking price tag. With the average Tampa Electric residential bill hovering around $175 [1], and much higher during the sweltering summer months, finding ways to reduce that cost is a top priority for many homeowners. The good news is you have more control over your energy consumption than you might think.

Your HVAC system is the single biggest energy user in your home. By making a few strategic adjustments and keeping your system in top shape, you can significantly lower your monthly energy bill without sacrificing comfort. Here are 7 HVAC tips that actually work for Tampa homeowners.

1. Set Your Thermostat to 78°F When You're Home

It might sound warm, but 78°F is the temperature recommended by FPL and other energy experts for optimal cooling and energy savings during Florida summers. For every single degree you set your thermostat below 78°F, you increase your cooling costs by approximately 3-5%. If you typically set it to 72°F, adjusting to 78°F could save you nearly 20-30% on your bill.

When you're away from home, you can save even more by setting the thermostat to a higher temperature, like 82-85°F. A smart thermostat can automate this process for you, which we'll cover in a bit.

2. Change Your Air Filter Every 30-60 Days

This is one of the simplest and most effective things you can do. In Tampa's dusty and humid environment, your AC's air filter gets clogged quickly. A dirty filter forces your HVAC system to work much harder to pull in air, which drives up energy use and puts unnecessary strain on the components.

A clean filter allows for smooth airflow, improving efficiency and indoor air quality. For most homes in our area, we recommend checking your filter every 30 days and replacing it at least every 60 days. If you have pets or allergy sufferers, you may need to change it more frequently. Need help finding the right filter? We can help with air filter replacement.

3. Seal Your Home's Ductwork

Did you know that the typical home loses 20-30% of the air that moves through its duct system due to leaks, holes, and poor connections? That's like running your AC with a window wide open. Your cooled air is escaping into the attic, crawl spaces, or walls instead of reaching your living areas.

Sealing your ductwork is one of the most impactful energy-saving improvements you can make. At On The Way Heating & Air, our technicians can perform professional duct sealing to ensure that cooled air gets where it needs to go, drastically improving your system's efficiency and lowering your bills.

4. Schedule Annual AC Tune-Ups

A well-maintained air conditioner can use 15-25% less energy than a neglected one. An annual AC tune-up is crucial for keeping your system running at peak performance. During a tune-up, our technicians clean the coils, check refrigerant levels, test electrical components, and calibrate your system for maximum efficiency.

This preventative maintenance not only saves you money on energy but also helps catch small problems before they become major, expensive breakdowns. It's the key to a reliable and efficient system. For year-round peace of mind and savings, consider joining our Comfort Club. It includes two tune-ups per year, discounts on repairs, and priority service. Learn more about our AC maintenance plans.

5. Upgrade to a Programmable or Smart Thermostat

If you still have an old manual thermostat, you're missing out on significant savings. A programmable thermostat allows you to "set it and forget it," automatically adjusting the temperature when you're asleep or away. A smart thermostat takes it a step further, learning your habits and allowing you to control your AC from your smartphone.

These devices ensure you're not needlessly cooling an empty house, which can cut your cooling costs by 10% or more. If your current thermostat is malfunctioning or outdated, our team can help with thermostat repair and installation.

6. Keep Your Outdoor Unit Clean and Clear

Your outdoor condenser unit is where your AC system releases the heat it has removed from your home. For it to work efficiently, it needs unrestricted airflow. Make sure to keep at least 2 feet of clearance around the unit on all sides. Trim back any bushes, weeds, or grass that may be crowding it.

It's also important to gently rinse the condenser fins with a garden hose once or twice a year to remove dirt, dust, and debris that can block airflow. A clean outdoor unit can operate much more efficiently, leading to lower energy consumption.

7. Consider Upgrading an Old AC System

If your AC system is over 10-12 years old, it may be time to consider an upgrade. HVAC technology has advanced dramatically, and modern units are significantly more efficient than their older counterparts. The efficiency of an AC unit is measured by its SEER (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio) rating—or the newer SEER2 standard. The higher the number, the more efficient the unit.

Upgrading from an old 10 SEER unit to a new 16 SEER2 unit could reduce your cooling costs by 30-50%. While the initial investment is a consideration, the long-term energy savings can be substantial.
